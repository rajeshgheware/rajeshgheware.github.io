faq:
  - q: Is cycling good training for trekking?
    a: >-
      Yes, for the aerobic base: long, steady hours that teach your body to keep going. It is low-impact, so you can
      do a lot of it. But it doesn't train walking uphill with a pack, steep descents or balance on rough ground, so
      combine it with stairs, step-ups and hikes.
  - q: How much cycling should I do before a Himalayan trek?
    a: >-
      There's no single number. Use long rides in the base phase, building up to rides of several hours, then switch
      the focus to stairs and loaded hiking in the last 6–8 weeks. My biggest summer on the bike (2023) averaged about 300 km a
      month, including a 200 km and a 400 km brevet.
  - q: Can cycling replace hiking practice?
    a: >-
      No. Cycling works your legs in a seated, smooth circle. Trekking means carrying your own weight and a pack,
      stepping up and down, and absorbing long descents. Your legs need that specific practice too.

## What my Strava log shows

I compared the riding I logged before three of my treks:

| Before | Period | Rides | Distance | Climbing | Biggest ride |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Everest Base Camp](/treks/everest-base-camp/) (Oct 2022) | Jun–Sep 2022 | 8 | 173 km | 1,758 m | |
| [Bodhpathri Lakes](/treks/bodhpathri-lakes/) (Aug–Sep 2023) | May–Aug 2023 | 19 | **1,251 km** | 12,174 m | [Puttaparthi 400 km BRM](/rides/puttaparthi-400-brm/) |
| [Kang Yatse 2](/treks/kang-yatse-2/) (Sep 2025) | Jun–Aug 2025 | 4 | **46 km** | 722 m | |

The summer of 2023 was my biggest block of endurance training: a [200 km night BRM](/rides/minchina-ota-200-brm/) in May
and a 400 km BRM in July. The summer of 2025 was almost empty on the bike. On Kang Yatse 2 I reached base camp already
out of energy, with my lower back flared up, and turned back on summit day. Running short of trail food played its part
(see [the Kang Yatse 2 post](/treks/kang-yatse-2/)), but a thin endurance base left me no reserve to fall back on.

[Bali Pass](/treks/bali-pass/) (2021) isn't in the table because my rides on Strava start in March 2022. For that trek
my base was [stair climbing](/treks/guides/high-altitude-fitness/).

## What cycling trains well

- **The aerobic engine.** A 200 km brevet is 11–12 hours of steady effort. That is the same "keep going all day" ability
  a 10-hour trek day needs.
- **Volume without pounding.** Riding is low-impact, so you can pile up hours that would be hard on your knees as running.
- **Eating and drinking on the move.** Long rides force you to practise fuelling, which is exactly what summit day needs.
  For my 400 km BRM I used honey mixed with an electrolyte drink, based on
  [Dr Andy Galpin's videos](https://www.youtube.com/@drandygalpin), and rode 27 hours without sleep. See
  [the Puttaparthi 400 post](/rides/puttaparthi-400-brm/).
- **Back-to-back days.** Multi-day tours like [TOK 9](/rides/tok-9/), the Tour of Karnataka, (504 km in four days) train you to recover overnight
  and go again.

## What cycling doesn't train

- **Climbing on foot with a pack.** Your legs work differently when they carry your whole weight up a step.
- **Descents.** Long downhills on foot load the front of your thighs and your knees in a way cycling never does.
- **Balance on rough ground,** such as boulders, scree and snow.
- **Carrying the pack all day.** Core and lower-back endurance needs its own work. See the strength section of my
  [training guide](/treks/guides/high-altitude-fitness/).

## How I'd combine them

1. **Base phase:** make long rides the main aerobic session each week, building to several hours.
2. **Build phase:** keep one long ride a week and add stairs or step-ups with a pack twice a week.
3. **Last 6–8 weeks:** shift the focus to the trek itself: stairs, loaded hikes and back-to-back days on foot. Keep a
   shorter ride for easy aerobic volume.
4. **Don't let the base lapse** in the months before a big trek. That is the mistake my 2025 log shows.
